# When a legal concern arrives: moderator playbook

This is a working checklist, not legal advice. It's meant to be followed in the
first hours, before you've spoken to a lawyer. Read it once now, so you aren't
reading it for the first time when a letter arrives.

The repository is public, so this file deliberately holds no names, contact
details or strategy beyond what's below.

## 1. What arrived?

| It looks like | Go to |
| --- | --- |
| A "Legal concern" report on the site (the review was hidden automatically) | step 2 |
| An email or letter from a property owner, manager or lawyer about a review | step 2 |
| Court papers, a subpoena, or an order to hand over information | step 5, and call a lawyer today |
| A copyright complaint about a picture or text | step 6 |
| A request about someone's personal information | step 7 |

## 2. First hour: protect the record, then the review

1. **Don't delete anything.** Not the review, the report, the email, the
   moderation history, a proof picture, or a reviewer's account. Deleting
   records after a claim is threatened can be held against you.
2. **Save the notice.** Screenshot or save the message with its date, time and
   sender. Note the exact date you first saw it.
3. **Hide the review** if it isn't already (a legal-concern report hides it
   automatically). On the Moderation page, use Unpublish and add a note saying
   why.
4. **Place a legal hold** on the review (Moderation page, under the review:
   "Place legal hold"). This stops the nightly cleanups from deleting the
   review, its history, its reports and its proof pictures. Add a note such as
   "Letter received on [date]".
5. **Don't reply with substance.** Don't argue, apologise, explain who wrote it
   or admit anything. If you reply at all, acknowledge receipt only, for
   example: "We've received your message and are looking into it. The review has
   been hidden while we do." Don't discuss it publicly or on social media.

## 3. Don't

- Don't reveal anything about who wrote a review (an account email, an IP
  fingerprint, a time of submission) to anyone who asks. Only a lawyer should
  deal with a court order for that (step 5).
- Don't change a review's meaning. Moderator edits only remove words, and the
  writer's original is kept.
- Don't restore a hidden review quickly to "show you're not intimidated". Wait
  for advice.
- Don't ignore deadlines. Court documents have short deadlines, often days to
  weeks. Act the day they arrive.

## 4. Get advice

- Call a lawyer who handles defamation or media law, and your insurer if you
  have a media liability policy (tell them straight away: some policies need
  prompt notice of a claim or a threat).
- The Legal Information Society of Nova Scotia gives free legal information and
  lawyer referrals (information, not advice about your situation).
- Give the lawyer: the notice, the review text, the moderation history (the
  Moderation page shows it), the report, and what the review's property page
  looked like.

## 5. Court orders and requests for a reviewer's identity

Courts can order a website to disclose who wrote something. If an order or a
request arrives:

- Put a legal hold on the review straight away and keep everything.
- Give it to your lawyer. Don't comply, and don't refuse, on your own.
- What the site can hold about a reviewer is limited: the account email, only if
  they were signed in when they submitted (reviews submitted without signing in
  have no link); a one-way IP fingerprint kept for about 48 hours (not
  reversible to an address); any email given in a report; and proof pictures
  (the browser removes location data before upload). The hosting providers
  (Supabase, Vercel, Cloudflare) keep their own technical logs.

## 6. Copyright complaints

Ask for what the work is, where it appears on the site, and who owns it. If a
picture or text plausibly infringes, hide the review and tell your lawyer. Keep
the record.

## 7. Privacy requests

Someone may ask what you hold about them, to correct it, or to delete it.
Reviews submitted without signing in can't be tied to a person, so ask which
property and what the review says. Reply within about 30 days. Account deletion
is available from the Account page and unlinks any reviews from the account.

## 8. After

- Record the outcome in the review's hold note.
- Release the hold only when your lawyer says the matter is closed. After that
  the normal retention rules apply again: rejected reviews are deleted 24 months
  after they came down, and closed reports 24 months after they were closed.
- If the review was wrong to publish, change the moderator checklist so the same
  thing is caught next time.

## Contacts to fill in privately

Keep these in a private place, not in this public repository: your lawyer's
details, your insurer and policy number, and a second person who can act if
you're unavailable.
